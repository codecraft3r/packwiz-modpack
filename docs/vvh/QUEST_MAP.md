## Campaign shape

The central spine is:

`Island Charter → Three Invitations → one of three Foundations → any 3/8 Specialty Projects → any 3/8 Island Works → Fair preparation → any 3/6 Fair contributions → Season Seal → limited postgame requisitions`

Side lanes deepen the world without blocking the spine. Post-charter faction projects, additional specialty projects, `Rivalry Without Ruin`, faction demonstrations, and postgame rumours can be pursued whenever their prerequisites appear. The rivalry lane branches after the specialty chapter and remains optional; both its archive and opt-in skirmish become available after any two noncombat formats.

The distinction is about progression, not importance: central-spine chapters carry the season toward the Fair, while side lanes create optional character, competition, and sequel hooks.

## Faction foundations

The three routes share a reward and effort budget while keeping different shapes:

1. one identity/current-state confirmation opener;
2. two visible internal lanes: progression and world-building (these are choices inside a foundation, not campaign side lanes);
3. a flexible charter gate—House and Lantern require any four of six works; Free Companies require any five of eight;
4. a shared charter reward with construction, school-support, and practical utility;
5. optional post-charter improvements that deepen the headquarters without delaying the campaign spine.

The routes are intentionally not palette-swapped copies. Vampires build around coffins, an altar, Blood-school study, Vampire's Delight hospitality, night routes, hosting, and Create utility. Their optional refuge arc adds a readable guest rule and an unclaimed-cup welcome. Hunters build around watchhouses, a Hunter Table, Holy wards, garlic reserves, alchemy, refuge routes, and safety drills; their optional arc refits, consecrates, and opens the refuge under treaty light. Neutrals build contracts, courier routes, markets, guest space, archives, MCA civic relationships, mediation/rescue, and a limited Blood/Holy translation desk, then may extend the contract hall with recovery, courier, and public-spellwright services.

## Specialty projects

Chapter 05 is a central-spine breadth gate, but each node is a distinct project rather than a generic personal chore. Engineer, Arcanist, Faction Specialist, Aeronaut, Diplomat, Quartermaster, Pathfinder, and Archivist projects each separate:

- **Lore:** why that craft matters to the island;
- **Objective:** the concrete item, action, or documented outcome;
- **Build standard:** what a peer or host must be able to verify.

Quest completion is recorded by the player's current FTB quest team. The resulting service, lesson, route, agreement, or facility may belong to a faction or the whole server. A specialty can support an existing foundation work, but it must add a new capability or documented improvement rather than merely relabel the same output. `THREE HANDS' WORTH` now correctly accepts any three of the eight specialties; Archivist is no longer an accidental mandatory fourth choice. Two optional mastery beats then ask a player to teach their method and leave it usable for somebody else.

## Island works and cooperative events

Chapter 06's Island Works are central-spine civic projects. They may grow out of faction and specialty work, but their audience is broader: another faction, a late joiner, or an unbriefed visitor must be able to use or understand them. Review standards therefore vary by project—navigation for a route, active offers for a market, exposed capabilities for a workshop, preserved facts for an archive, and so on—instead of relying on one repeated sentence.

Chapters 07 and 08 contain cooperative events, not inventory gates. Rivalry formats require shared rules, consent where relevant, a visible ending, and cleanup. Fair preparation establishes the shared venue before contributions open. Fair contributions require one hosted output that gives other players something to visit, use, judge, learn, or do. Attendance and participation create the story; winning never grants progression power. A post-seal archive quest records what the Fair changed without blocking completion.

## After the Bells

Chapter 09 turns Bevels into visible convenience rather than runaway power. Seven team-scoped weekly requisitions cost ten Bevels in total and supply lighting, transit, festival, repair, hospitality, public-works, and low-tier neutral spellcraft materials. `ARCHIVE A NEW RUMOUR` remains the only repeatable currency source at one team Bevel per seven days, so it cannot self-fund the board.

## Minimum mainline

The campaign uses flexible progression and native `min_required_dependencies` rather than forcing completionism. A minimum route includes:

- Charter signature
- one foundation opener + its flexible foundation threshold + foundation charter
- specialty opener + any three specialty projects + specialty completion
- Island Works opener + any three civic projects + civic completion
- Fair opener + Fair preparation + any three contributions + Season One seal

Optional reference clauses, extra foundation works, extra specialty projects, the rivalry side lane, skirmish, and postgame rumours are not required for the seal.
