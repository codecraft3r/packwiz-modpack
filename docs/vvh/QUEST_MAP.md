## Campaign shape

The central spine is:

`Island Charter → Three Invitations → one of three Foundations → any 3/8 Specialty Projects → any 3/8 Island Works → Long Night Fair → limited postgame requisitions`

Side lanes deepen the world without blocking the spine. Extra foundation works, additional specialty projects, `Rivalry Without Ruin`, faction demonstrations, and postgame rumours can be pursued whenever their prerequisites appear. The rivalry lane branches after the specialty chapter and remains optional; its skirmish node requires two completed noncombat formats.

The distinction is about progression, not importance: central-spine chapters carry the season toward the Fair, while side lanes create optional character, competition, and sequel hooks.

## Foundation equivalence

Each of House of Night, Lantern Order, and Free Companies uses the same structural budget:

1. one identity/current-state confirmation opener;
2. two visible internal lanes: progression and world-building (these are choices inside a foundation, not campaign side lanes);
3. eight foundation works;
4. complete any five;
5. name one work from each lane, the headquarters, and a maintenance owner;
6. receive 32 Scaffolding, 4 Super Glue, a modest school-support supply, and one practical choice cache.

The routes are intentionally not palette-swapped copies. Vampires build around coffins, an altar, Blood-school study, Vampire's Delight hospitality, night routes, hosting, and Create utility. Hunters build around watchhouses, a Hunter Table, Holy wards, garlic reserves, alchemy, refuge routes, and safety drills. Neutrals build contracts, courier routes, markets, guest space, archives, MCA civic relationships, mediation/rescue, and a limited Blood/Holy translation desk.

## Specialty projects

Chapter 05 is a central-spine breadth gate, but each node is a distinct project rather than a generic personal chore. Engineer, Arcanist, Faction Specialist, Aeronaut, Diplomat, Quartermaster, Pathfinder, and Archivist projects each separate:

- **Lore:** why that craft matters to the island;
- **Objective:** the concrete item, action, or documented outcome;
- **Build standard:** what a peer or host must be able to verify.

Quest completion is recorded by the player's current FTB quest team. The resulting service, lesson, route, agreement, or facility may belong to a faction or the whole server. A specialty can support an existing foundation work, but it must add a new capability or documented improvement rather than merely relabel the same output.

## Island works and cooperative events

Chapter 06's Island Works are central-spine civic projects. They may grow out of faction and specialty work, but their audience is broader: another faction, a late joiner, or an unbriefed visitor must be able to use or understand them. Review standards therefore vary by project—navigation for a route, active offers for a market, exposed capabilities for a workshop, preserved facts for an archive, and so on—instead of relying on one repeated sentence.

Chapters 07 and 08 contain cooperative events, not inventory gates. Rivalry formats require shared rules, consent where relevant, a visible ending, and cleanup. Fair contributions require one hosted output that gives other players something to visit, use, judge, learn, or do. Attendance and participation create the story; winning never grants progression power.

## Minimum mainline

The campaign uses flexible progression and native `min_required_dependencies` rather than forcing completionism. A minimum route includes:

- Charter signature
- one foundation opener + any five foundation works + foundation charter
- specialty opener + any three specialty projects + specialty completion
- Island Works opener + any three civic projects + civic completion
- Fair opener + any three contributions + Season One seal

Optional reference clauses, extra foundation works, extra specialty projects, the rivalry side lane, skirmish, and postgame rumours are not required for the seal.
